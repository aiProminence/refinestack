# Market evidence: Single Brain and 11x

**Research date:** 16 August 2026  
**Scope:** Current user reviews, independent reporting, pricing commentary, implementation friction, security/privacy concerns, edge cases, and competitive patterns. Product documentation inventories are included only where needed to validate market claims.

## Executive findings

1. **11x's strongest perceived value is not full autonomy.** It is consolidation, scale, faster follow-up, usable campaign creation, and unusually hands-on customer success.
2. **Its largest risk is the gap between the promise and operational reality.** Detailed complaints cite incorrect targeting, malformed or generic messages, hallucinations, stalled sending, outdated data, intensive human monitoring, deliverability problems, and weak outcomes.
3. **Positive review evidence is directionally useful but potentially selection-biased.** 11x asks customers to review on Trustpilot, several G2 reviews are seller-invited or incentivized, and many positive reviews emphasize a named customer-success manager or early onboarding rather than long-term attributed revenue.
4. **Single Brain is currently closer to a managed-service proposition than independently proven software.** Its strongest concepts are one conversational interface, a specialist-agent fleet, workflow execution inside Slack or Teams, and isolated deployments. No credible independent Single Brain product reviews or analyst evaluations were located during this research.
5. **Single Brain's own founder provides the most candid risk evidence.** Eric Siu has publicly stated that their agents break, hallucinate, and cost real money to operate. This sits in tension with the polished site promise that agents do not drift or need constant rescue.
6. **RefineStack should compete on demonstrable truth rather than maximum autonomy:** provenance, reproducible metrics, honest confidence, observable work, tenant isolation, human approvals, failure recovery, and transparent limits.

## Evidence gap: Single Brain

No credible independent product reviews, analyst evaluations, public pricing, SLA documents, public trust-center evidence, or production case studies with reproducible methodology were located for Single Brain as of the research date. The vendor itself labels SOC 2 Type II as in progress. Single Brain should therefore be treated as a useful product and service thesis, not as a validated performance benchmark.

The strongest transferable ideas are:

- one trusted interaction surface;
- specialist execution behind a coordinating layer;
- work inside existing collaboration tools;
- per-client isolation;
- clear status and audit trails;
- a narrow first deployment with explicit go/no-go qualification.

## Competitive patterns

- “Autonomous” products are often high-touch managed services in practice.
- Human expertise remains the hidden success layer.
- Value is highest when the buyer already has a defined market, offer, owner, and working process.
- Automation exposes weak strategy rather than automatically repairing it.
- Large-volume use cases tolerate imperfect precision better than narrow, high-value markets.
- Pricing is moving toward annual enterprise contracts while buyers still want proof before commitment.
- Aggregate review scores obscure a polarized distribution: enthusiastic supported users and a smaller group reporting severe outcome or contract failures.
- Trust, attribution, and observability are more durable differentiation than agent personalities.
- A conversational interface reduces interface burden, but a dashboard remains necessary for auditability, comparison, and governance.

## Concrete RefineStack opportunities

### 1. Evidence-first intelligence

Every recommendation observation must retain the raw provider response, request/prompt version, timestamp, model, access method, geography/market, citations, and classification explanation. Never manufacture evidence to complete a record.

### 2. Reproducible scoring

AI Recommendation Share and related metrics must expose their denominator, eligible and failed captures, weighting, algorithm version, and confidence. Reclassifying old captures must create a new version rather than silently rewriting history.

### 3. Visible work state

Every run needs queued, running, partially complete, blocked, failed, cancelled, and complete states, with progress by AI surface. A run must not appear complete when one provider failed silently.

### 4. Human approval at consequential points

Users should review competitor selection, buyer-decision questions, and low-confidence classifications. Low-confidence output must enter a review queue rather than being silently accepted into metrics.

### 5. Guided onboarding that substitutes for a customer-success manager

Include domain validation, category/market confirmation, competitor suggestions with reasons, question-quality scoring, sample captures, and a readiness check before consuming a paid run. Record the accountable workspace owner.

### 6. Source freshness and conflict handling

Expose publication/capture dates, redirects, inaccessible sources, contradictory evidence, duplicate citations, and claims older than a configurable threshold.

### 7. Honest action recommendations

Separate observed evidence from inference. Each proposed intervention should link to affected decisions and explain expected impact, effort, uncertainty, and the evidence that would confirm improvement.

### 8. Enterprise-grade tenancy from the beginning

Use workspace-scoped queries, role permissions, auditable mutations, expiring invitations, secure secret storage, support-access consent, export/delete workflows, and automated cross-tenant tests.

### 9. Transparent usage and limits

Show projected cost before a run, provider consumption, remaining allowance, retry charging policy, and hard spend caps. Failed captures must remain auditable and must not quietly inflate score denominators.

### 10. Credible commercial proof

Distinguish simulations, beta pilots, active production customers, and former customers. Require recorded consent before publishing a company name, logo, testimonial, or result.

### 11. Dual-mode product experience

Borrow Single Brain's low-friction conversational front door for questions and guided actions, but retain RefineStack's dashboard for evidence inspection, time-series comparison, coverage, audit history, and configuration.

### 12. Honest product positioning

Do not promise that AI never fails. Promise that every result is observable, attributable, governable, and recoverable. Public marketing claims must map to functioning capabilities or be clearly labeled planned/private beta.

## Adversarial acceptance scenarios

### Accuracy, classification, and provenance

- A provider recommends a competitor but supplies no citation. RefineStack stores the answer, marks evidence confidence as low, and never fabricates a supporting source.
- Two providers disagree on the preferred brand. The UI preserves both results and computes a transparent aggregate rather than forcing false consensus.
- The same raw captures are reclassified after an algorithm update. Historical scores remain immutable; the new result is separately versioned and reproducible.
- A provider returns malformed JSON, Markdown instead of structured output, an unexpected language, an empty response, or a refusal. The adapter recovers safely or records an explicit failure.
- A crawled page contains prompt injection instructing the evaluator to ignore policy, alter scores, or reveal secrets. Page content is treated as untrusted data and cannot modify system behavior.
- A source repeats one self-published claim across syndicated pages. Duplicate evidence is detected and is not counted as multiple independent authority signals.
- An answer mentions a brand without recommending it. Mention, citation, explicit recommendation, and first-choice recommendation remain separate classified events.
- A provider's API response differs from a personalized consumer session. The product labels the access method accurately and does not imply consumer-session equivalence.

### Metrics and comparisons

- A metric appears to improve only because failed provider runs were excluded. Coverage change is displayed beside the score and the comparison is flagged as potentially misleading.
- Two date ranges use different questions, markets, provider mixes, or model versions. The interface warns they are not directly comparable and identifies every changed dimension.
- All captures fail. The UI shows “unavailable” with failure reasons, never zero recommendation share.
- Only one of three providers succeeds. The denominator includes only the successful capture, while coverage clearly reads 1/3 and confidence is reduced.
- Re-running stored captures through the same classifier version produces the same results and score.
- Rounding cannot cause competitor shares to exceed 100%, hide a tie, or change rank ordering.

### Jobs, limits, and failure recovery

- One provider times out while the others succeed. The run becomes partially complete, reports the correct denominator, and offers a safe provider-specific retry.
- A user double-clicks Run, refreshes, or repeats a request after a network timeout. Exactly one billable job is created.
- A provider rate-limits the system for several hours. Retry-after and exponential backoff work without duplicate capture records, with visible next-retry status.
- A run reaches its approved spend cap. Further calls stop and completed results remain usable.
- A scheduled run overlaps a still-running prior job. The system prevents, coalesces, or safely sequences the duplicate.
- A worker crashes after the provider charged but before persistence completes. Idempotency prevents a second charge/call when the job resumes.
- A domain blocks crawling, loops redirects, requires JavaScript, serves a soft 404, or varies content by geography/device. Each receives a distinct diagnostic.

### Tenant isolation and authorization

- A user changes a workspace ID in a URL, GraphQL variable, API body, or storage path. No object, count, filename, job state, or timing signal from another tenant is revealed.
- A viewer attempts to edit competitors, launch captures, invite users, change billing, or access integration secrets. Every action is rejected server-side.
- A removed member retains an old session and direct API URLs. Workspace access is revoked immediately.
- An invitation is reused, forwarded, expired, or accepted using another email address. It fails safely and is audit-logged.
- Two workspaces monitor the same company. Their questions, notes, evidence, reports, actions, and schedules remain isolated.
- Support access requires explicit, time-limited customer approval and creates immutable entry, activity, and exit audit records.

### Privacy, security, and lifecycle

- A user disconnects an AI or analytics provider. Tokens are revoked/deleted, dependent schedules stop, and deletion is recorded without revealing secret values.
- Workspace deletion removes captures, exports, files, and derived embeddings after the documented retention window.
- Secrets never appear in client bundles, URLs, browser logs, exports, error messages, analytics events, support transcripts, or provider prompts.
- Data export includes customer-owned configuration, captures, classifications, notes, and audit history without exposing platform secrets or another tenant's data.
- An uploaded document contains personal or sensitive data. Access, retention, processing, and deletion rules apply consistently to raw and derived records.
- Account takeover attempts trigger rate limiting, secure session rotation, and appropriate notifications without disclosing whether an email address is registered.
- An administrator reduces a member's role during an active session. The next protected operation uses current authorization, not stale client state.

### Trust and commercial integrity

- A pilot workspace is selected for a public customer page without recorded consent. Publication is blocked.
- A former customer or expired pilot remains in a logo list. Status rules detect the change and require removal/re-approval.
- A recommendation is based primarily on the customer's own website. The system labels source concentration and does not describe it as independent market authority.
- A user deletes a poor historical result. The product supports an audited exclusion or annotation, not silent history rewriting.
- A report omits failed providers, low-confidence results, or changed questions. Export validation blocks the misleading report or includes mandatory disclosure.
- A pricing estimate changes because provider rates or question counts changed. The user must see and confirm the revised estimate before new spend occurs.

### Accessibility, device, and input resilience

- A user completes onboarding, question review, run monitoring, evidence inspection, and report export using keyboard only and a screen reader.
- Tables and charts expose equivalent textual values, meaningful labels, focus order, and status distinctions that do not depend on color.
- Long brand names, international domains, Unicode, multilingual text, right-to-left content, 200% zoom, narrow mobile widths, and large evidence sets remain usable.
- Slow mobile connectivity, browser back/forward navigation, or offline interruption does not duplicate actions or lose confirmed input.
- Questions containing quotes, markup, emoji, long URLs, SQL-like text, or prompt-injection phrases are stored and rendered safely without becoming executable instructions.

## Product principle

> RefineStack should not promise that AI never fails. It should make every AI result observable, attributable, governable, and recoverable.
